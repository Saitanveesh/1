## 3. What a future license choice would mean

| Option | Broad effect | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **All rights reserved (current)** | Owner controls copying and distribution | Not OSI-open-source; permission needed for most reuse |
| MIT | Permissive commercial and noncommercial reuse, retain copyright notice | Broad irreversible grants for the version released |
| Apache-2.0 | Permissive reuse with express patent-related terms | Notice and compliance requirements |
| GPLv3 | Copyleft obligations on qualifying redistribution | Distribution/integration complexity |
| AGPLv3 | Network-use source availability obligations for qualifying modified versions | SaaS/commercial trade-offs |
| Dual or commercial license | Separate sets of rights for different audiences | Requires ownership clarity and legal drafting |

**No open-source option has been chosen.** Before a permissive license is introduced, audit the repository's original authors, upstream code, dependency licenses, third-party assets and contributor consents. A public repo with an all-rights-reserved LICENSE may not be described as open-source.

## 5. Before public binary releases

Review authorship and contributor agreements; inventory direct/transitive dependencies, embedded assets and notices; choose a license explicitly; inspect copyleft compatibility; verify test/lab data provenance; obtain legal review for commercial offerings.
